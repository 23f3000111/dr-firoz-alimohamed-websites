Hepatitis and liver disease

Inflammation of the liver from viruses, alcohol, medicines or fat. It is often silent for years, showing up first as an abnormal blood test, and it is far easier to treat when found early.

Patient brochurePDF download, available soon

What it is

Hepatitis means inflammation of the liver. The common causes are viral infection, especially hepatitis B and C, alcohol, fat building up in the liver, and some medicines and herbal remedies. If inflammation continues for years it can scar the liver, a process called fibrosis, and eventually lead to cirrhosis.

Symptoms

  • Often none at all
  • Tiredness and poor appetite
  • Discomfort in the upper right abdomen
  • Jaundice: yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, pale stools
  • Itching

How it is found

Blood tests measure liver function and look for viral infection and other causes. Fibroscan shows how much scarring and fat the liver holds, and ultrasound shows its structure along with the gallbladder and bile ducts. Liver biopsy is kept for cases where the diagnosis still needs it.

How it is treated

Treatment follows the cause. Antiviral medicines can cure hepatitis C and control hepatitis B. Fatty liver responds to weight loss and better control of diabetes and cholesterol. Alcohol related disease improves when drinking stops, and a medicine causing harm is stopped. Follow up tracks the liver over time, and vaccination protects against hepatitis A and B.

If it is urgent

New jaundice, confusion, vomiting blood or a rapidly swelling abdomen need same day assessment. Go to the hospital rather than waiting for a clinic appointment.
