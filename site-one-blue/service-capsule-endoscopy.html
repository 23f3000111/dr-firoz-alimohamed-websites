Capsule endoscopy

A simple way to examine your small intestine: a modern, noninvasive test to help investigate digestive problems.

Book a consultation

What is capsule endoscopy?

Capsule endoscopy is a test that lets your doctor look at the inside of your small intestine. This part of the digestive system can be difficult to examine with a standard endoscopy or colonoscopy.

You swallow a small capsule, about the size of a large vitamin pill. It contains a tiny camera and light. As the capsule moves naturally through your digestive system, it takes thousands of pictures of the lining of your small intestine. The pictures are sent to a small recording device and reviewed by your doctor.

You do not need to have an endoscope passed through your mouth or anus for this test.

Why might I need capsule endoscopy?

Your doctor may suggest capsule endoscopy if you have:

  • Unexplained anaemia: Especially if you have low iron levels that continue despite normal initial tests.
  • Possible bleeding in the digestive tract: When a gastroscopy and colonoscopy have not found the cause.
  • Possible Crohn’s disease: To look for inflammation in the small intestine when this test is suitable for you.
  • Possible problems in the small intestine: Such as ulcers, polyps or other changes that may not be found with standard endoscopy.

What happens during the procedure?

Capsule endoscopy is usually simple:

  1. You follow the preparation instructions from your healthcare team. These may include fasting and drinking a bowel cleansing solution.
  2. You swallow the capsule with water.
  3. You wear a small recording device while the capsule takes pictures as it travels through your digestive system.
  4. You can usually carry out light daily activities, as advised by your healthcare team.
  5. The capsule passes naturally in your stool, usually within a few days.

The test is generally painless and usually does not require sedation. The capsule is for single use and does not need to be collected.

Is capsule endoscopy safe?

Capsule endoscopy is generally safe and well tolerated. In some people, however, the capsule can become stuck in a narrowed part of the intestine. This is called capsule retention.

Tell your doctor if you have ever had a bowel blockage, a narrowed intestine, Crohn’s disease or surgery on your intestine. You may need extra tests before having capsule endoscopy.

The capsule can take pictures, but it cannot take tissue samples or remove polyps. If the test finds an abnormality, you may need further tests or treatment.
