Oesophageal pH and impedance studies

Understanding your acid reflux: advanced tests to help diagnose reflux and investigate persistent symptoms.

Book a consultation

What are oesophageal pH and impedance studies?

Oesophageal pH and impedance studies are specialised tests that measure how often stomach contents flow backwards into the food pipe (oesophagus).

They help doctors investigate gastro oesophageal reflux disease (GERD), commonly known as acid reflux, particularly when symptoms persist despite treatment or the diagnosis is uncertain.

The tests can detect acid reflux and, using impedance measurements, identify reflux that is weakly acidic or nonacidic. They can also help assess whether reflux episodes are associated with symptoms such as heartburn, regurgitation or chronic cough.

Why might I need this test?

Your doctor may recommend oesophageal pH and impedance monitoring if you have:

  • Persistent heartburn or acid regurgitation.
  • Symptoms that continue despite acid suppressing medication.
  • Unexplained chronic cough or throat symptoms where reflux is suspected.
  • A need to confirm GERD before considering certain treatments or surgery.
  • Symptoms despite normal findings on a previous endoscopy.

How is the test performed?

A thin, flexible tube containing special sensors is gently passed through your nose into the oesophagus. The tube is connected to a small recording device that you carry with you.

In some cases, a small wireless pH capsule attached to the oesophageal lining during an endoscopy may be used to measure acid exposure, although this does not provide the same impedance measurements as a study using a catheter.

During monitoring, you can usually continue your normal daily activities. You may be asked to record when you eat, lie down, experience symptoms or take medication.

The monitoring period is commonly around 24 hours for pH and impedance studies using a catheter.

Is the procedure uncomfortable?

You may experience mild discomfort in your nose or throat while the tube is in place. Most people can eat, drink and carry out their usual activities, subject to the instructions provided by their healthcare team.

The test does not usually require sedation.

How should I prepare?

Your doctor will explain whether you should stop acid suppressing medication before the test or continue taking it. This depends on whether the aim is to establish a diagnosis of GERD or investigate symptoms despite treatment.

Do not stop your medication unless instructed by your doctor.

What do the results show?

The study records how much acid reaches the oesophagus, how frequently reflux occurs and whether reflux episodes correspond with your symptoms.

The results help your doctor determine whether acid or nonacid reflux may be contributing to your symptoms and guide decisions about medication, further investigations or other treatment options.

If you have persistent heartburn, regurgitation or unexplained symptoms that may be related to reflux, speak to your doctor about whether oesophageal pH and impedance monitoring is appropriate for you.
