Capsule endoscopy

A tiny camera inside a capsule, swallowed with water. It photographs the small bowel, the long middle stretch of the gut that gastroscopy and colonoscopy cannot fully reach.

Book a consultation

What it is

The capsule is about the size of a large vitamin tablet and holds a camera, a light and a battery. As it travels through the gut it sends thousands of images to a small recorder you wear during the test. There is no sedation and no tube.

What it is used for

  • Bleeding or iron deficiency anaemia with no cause found on gastroscopy and colonoscopy
  • Suspected or known Crohn's disease of the small bowel
  • Suspected small bowel polyps or tumours
  • Coeliac disease that is not responding to treatment

How to prepare

You will be given written instructions. They usually include clear fluids only the day before, nothing to eat or drink overnight, and stopping iron tablets about a week before.

Tell the clinic first if you have had bowel surgery or a blockage, have difficulty swallowing, have a pacemaker, or are pregnant.

Afterwards

The capsule is disposable and passes naturally, usually within a day or two, so you do not need to retrieve it. Rarely, a capsule lodges at a narrowing in the bowel, which is why any history of blockage is checked first. Avoid MRI scans until it has passed.

What happens on the day

  1. 1

    Swallowing the capsule

    In the morning the recorder is fitted and you swallow the capsule with a little water.

  2. 2

    Going about your day

    You can leave the clinic and carry on with light activity. You will be told when you can drink and eat again.

  3. 3

    Returning the recorder

    Later the same day you return the recorder, so that the images can be downloaded.

  4. 4

    Results

    Dr. Firoz reviews the images and explains the findings at a follow up appointment.
