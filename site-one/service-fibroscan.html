Fibroscan

A quick, painless scan that measures how stiff the liver is and how much fat it holds. It gives a clear picture of liver scarring without a needle.

Book a consultation

What it is

Fibroscan is a specialised form of ultrasound. A probe resting on the skin over the liver sends a gentle pulse through the tissue and measures how fast it travels. The stiffer the liver, the more scarring it is likely to contain. The same examination also estimates how much fat the liver holds.

What it is used for

  • Fatty liver disease
  • Chronic hepatitis B and C
  • Alcohol related liver disease
  • Abnormal liver blood tests
  • Following liver scarring over time

In many people it reduces the need for a liver biopsy.

How to prepare

Nothing to eat or drink for about three hours before the scan, because a recent meal can change the reading. Keep taking your usual medicines unless told otherwise, and wear loose clothing.

Tell the clinic first if you are pregnant or have a pacemaker.

Reading the result

A Fibroscan score is read together with your blood tests and history. Recent meals, active hepatitis and fluid in the abdomen can all affect it, which is why one number is never taken alone.

What happens at the scan

  1. 1

    Lying down

    You lie on your back with your right arm raised behind your head.

  2. 2

    The probe

    A little gel goes on the skin over the liver, and the probe rests between your ribs.

  3. 3

    The readings

    You feel a light tap on the skin with each reading. There are no needles and no sedation.

  4. 4

    Results

    The result is explained alongside your blood tests, and you can go straight back to your day.
