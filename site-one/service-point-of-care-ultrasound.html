Point of care ultrasound for the liver

A quick, noninvasive assessment: a bedside imaging test to help evaluate liver health and guide medical care.

Book a consultation

What is point of care ultrasound for the liver?

Point of care ultrasound, often called POCUS, is a bedside imaging test that uses sound waves to create real time pictures of the liver and nearby organs.

A healthcare professional places a small handheld probe, called a transducer, on your upper abdomen. The probe sends sound waves into the body and produces images on an ultrasound machine or portable device.

Liver POCUS is noninvasive, does not use radiation and is usually quick and painless. It can be performed in a clinic, hospital, emergency department or other healthcare setting.

Why might I need a liver ultrasound?

Your healthcare professional may recommend liver POCUS if you have:

  • Abdominal pain: Particularly pain in the upper right side of your abdomen.
  • Abnormal liver blood tests: To look for possible changes in the liver or bile ducts.
  • Suspected liver disease: Such as fatty liver, cirrhosis or inflammation.
  • Possible fluid in the abdomen: Also called ascites.
  • Yellowing of the skin or eyes: Known as jaundice.
  • Concern about gallbladder or bile duct problems: These structures may also be assessed during the examination.
  • A need for rapid assessment: POCUS can help guide immediate decisions while further tests are arranged.

What can point of care ultrasound show?

Depending on the examination and the equipment used, liver POCUS may help assess:

  • The size and general appearance of the liver.
  • Changes in liver texture that may suggest chronic liver disease.
  • Fatty changes in the liver.
  • Fluid around or within the abdomen.
  • Enlargement of nearby blood vessels.
  • The gallbladder and bile ducts.
  • Large cysts, masses or other visible abnormalities.

Liver POCUS may also be used to guide procedures, such as draining abdominal fluid or taking a tissue sample, when appropriate.

What happens during the examination?

A liver ultrasound is usually straightforward:

  1. You may be asked to lie on your back or turn slightly onto your side.
  2. You may need to expose the upper part of your abdomen.
  3. The healthcare professional applies a clear gel to your skin.
  4. They move the ultrasound probe over your abdomen to obtain images.
  5. You may be asked to hold your breath briefly or change position.
  6. The examination usually takes only a few minutes, although the time can vary.

The gel may feel cool, and you may feel mild pressure from the probe. Most people do not experience pain.

Do I need to prepare?

Preparation depends on the reason for the examination and your healthcare provider’s instructions. You may be asked to avoid eating or drinking for several hours beforehand, as an empty stomach can sometimes improve the images.

Follow the instructions provided to you. If you take regular medication or have diabetes, ask your healthcare team whether you should make any changes before the examination.

Is liver point of care ultrasound safe?

Liver POCUS is generally considered safe. It does not use ionising radiation and can be repeated when clinically necessary.

However, it has limitations. The quality of the images may be affected by body size, bowel gas, scarring or difficulty holding your breath. POCUS may identify a possible abnormality, but it may not provide a complete diagnosis.

Your healthcare professional may recommend additional tests, such as formal ultrasound, blood tests, CT, MRI or specialist review, depending on the findings and your symptoms.
