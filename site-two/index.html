The examination, end to end

Four steps, one afternoon

  1. 01 Consultation

    Your account first

    When the symptom started, what changes it, what you have already tried, and every medicine you take. Most of the diagnosis is decided here; the camera only confirms it.

  2. 02 Preparation

    Fasting, and light sedation

    You are given written instructions for what to stop and when. On the day, sedation is given through a vein and you are monitored throughout. Most people remember very little of the examination itself.

  3. 03 The examination

    Looking, and treating

    Fifteen to forty minutes. The lining of the gut is inspected directly, biopsies are taken where needed, polyps are removed as they are found and bleeding points are sealed. Diagnosis and treatment in the same sitting.

  4. 04 Findings

    Told plainly, written down

    Once the sedation wears off you hear what was seen, in language you can repeat at home, with a written report. Biopsy results follow in about a week and are discussed at a follow up.

Conditions

The five seen most often

Bloating, heartburn, abdominal pain, constipation and jaundice are symptoms, not diagnoses. These are what they usually turn out to be.

  • Stomach & duodenum

    Peptic ulcers

    Sores in the stomach or duodenal lining causing burning upper abdominal pain. Most are caused by H. pylori or long term anti inflammatory tablets. Both are treatable, and both are worth confirming before treatment begins.

  • Whole bowel

    Irritable bowel syndrome

    Pain, bloating and a changed bowel habit with a bowel wall that looks normal. Real and common, and diagnosed properly by first excluding coeliac disease and inflammatory bowel disease.

  • Immune inflammation

    Inflammatory bowel disease

    Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis ulcerate the bowel wall. Confirmed by endoscopy and biopsy, then treated to reach remission and hold it, monitored rather than left until the next flare.

  • Screening matters

    Colon cancer

    Almost all colon cancers begin as silent polyps. Colonoscopy finds and removes them in the same session, which is why screening prevents the disease rather than only detecting it.

  • Liver

    Hepatitis

    Liver inflammation from viruses, alcohol, medicines or fat. Frequently silent until an abnormal blood test appears. Naming the cause is the whole task, and most causes have treatment.

  • Everything else

    Bring the symptom

    Reflux, coeliac disease, gallstones, pancreatitis, swallowing difficulty and unexplained anaemia are all routine work. Describe what you feel. Naming it is the specialist's job.

Training

Istanbul, Nairobi, Johannesburg

The undergraduate medical degree came from Istanbul University, the postgraduate degree in Internal Medicine from the University of Nairobi, and fellowship training in Gastroenterology from the University of the Witwatersrand in South Africa.

That sequence is the point. Internal medicine teaches you to read the whole patient; gastroenterology narrows to one system without losing the wider view. That is how a digestive complaint caused by the thyroid, a medicine or the heart gets caught.
